Olden Days Discipline vs Gen Z Freedom

Posted May 4, 2026 | Category: Olden Days vs New Era

My uncle sat me down last week with that look. You know the one. “In our days,” he started, “if you came home after 7pm, your legs would tell you a story. Koboko no dey ask questions.”

I laughed. Then I showed him my phone. My 16-year-old cousin had just texted her mum: “Mommy I’ll be home by 9:30, we’re finishing project. Love u 😘”. And her mum replied: “Ok dear, be safe. Send location.”

Two different worlds. Same Lagos. Who really had it better? Let’s talk about what we gained and what we lost.

The Fear vs The Negotiation

Back then, discipline was physical and instant. You feared your parents because consequences landed on your skin. Curfew was law, not a suggestion. One slap reset your whole week.

Today, Gen Z negotiates. They present evidence, appeal to logic, promise good grades. The koboko got replaced by “I’m not angry, just disappointed.” Parents now want to be friends first, wardens second.

What We Gained

1. Voice: Kids learn to argue, to reason, to set boundaries. They grow up knowing how to speak up at work, in relationships, with authority.

2. Trust: Fear-based obedience breaks when parents aren’t watching. Negotiated trust builds self-control. A kid who comes home because they agreed to, not because they’re scared, is stronger long term.

3. Mental space: No flinching at every raised hand. No anxiety from hiding mistakes. Gen Z talks to therapists. Our uncles talked to nobody and called it “being a man”.

What We Lost

1. Clear lines: When punishment was swift, nonsense stopped fast. You knew exactly where the wall was. Now the wall moves. “Can I go to this party?” becomes a TED Talk with PowerPoint points.

2. Instant respect: Fear made respect automatic. You didn’t roll your eyes at Dad in the 80s. Now respect is earned, questioned, sometimes ignored. That slows down homes.

3. Grit: Olden days kids were hard. Hunger, beating, “no” as a full sentence. It built calluses. I see 20-year-olds today who’ve never heard “no” collapse when life says it.

The Balance Sheet

Gained: Voice, negotiation, mental safety, kids who can challenge bad authority.

Lost: Clear boundaries, swift discipline, respect that didn’t need a debate, kids who could take a hit and keep moving.

So back to the question: discipline or freedom? Maybe the answer is both. Old school respect + new school negotiation. Hard kids become smart adults. Smart kids still need edges. The goal isn’t to bring back koboko. It’s to raise kids who don’t need it.

Next time my cousin negotiates curfew, I’ll tell her: “Your grandpa would’ve gotten koboko. You got conversation. Don’t waste it.”
