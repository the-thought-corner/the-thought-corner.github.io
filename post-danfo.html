The Small Joys: Danfo Conversations

Posted April 28, 2026

Nothing beats the gist you hear in a Lagos danfo. From politics to “that woman that waka pass”, everyday life still has flavor AI can’t replace.

I entered a danfo from CMS to Oshodi yesterday. Fare was ₦800. By the time we reached Maryland, I had learned 3 things: Tinubu’s cousin’s wife’s nephew is controlling rice prices, one passenger’s landlord is a “wizard”, and the conductor has been single for 7 years “because Lagos women like money pass love”.

No ChatGPT can give you that. No algorithm can predict it.

The University of the Streets

Danfo is Nigeria’s unofficial podcast. No subscription needed. No earphones. You get financial advice, political analysis, relationship counseling, and football arguments all in 45 minutes of traffic.

Last week, a woman in front was on the phone shouting: “I told you not to send me ‘k’! What is ‘k’? Are you a motor park boy? Type ‘okay’ like a responsible man!” The whole bus died. Even the driver smiled. That’s therapy.

Why It Matters in the AI Era

We’re outsourcing thinking to AI. Asking bots to write love letters, business plans, apologies to our wives. But danfo reminds you: humans are messy, loud, unpredictable. And that’s the point.

AI gives you perfect answers. Danfo gives you human answers. One passenger will say “Leave that girl, she’s not your destiny.” Another will say “Fight for love, my brother.” Both are wrong. Both are right. You have to choose. That’s life.

The Things We’ll Lose If We Stop Listening

1. Pidgin philosophy: “No be say you too small, na the bed too big.” Try explaining that to Claude.

2. Shared suffering: When rain enters through the window and everybody shifts at once, no one is a stranger. You’re all just Lagosians trying not to get wet.

3. Raw truth: An old man will look at your bleached skin and say “You don fry like plantain” to your face. No filter. No cancel culture. Just village council energy.

So next time you’re in a danfo, put your phone down. Listen. The algorithm of the streets is still undefeated. And the subscription is free — just ₦800 from CMS.
